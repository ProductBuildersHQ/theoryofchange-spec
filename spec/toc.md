# Theory of Change specification template

Use this outline to prepare or review a Theory of Change. Each section says
what to write and where it lives in the document. The structured document is
the source of truth; this outline is what a reader, or a judge, expects to find
told in prose.

## 1. Problem and context

- The problem in one paragraph, and who experiences it (`context.problemStatement`, `context.targetPopulation`).
- The causes, from immediate to structural, and which are out of scope and why (`causes`).
- The priority groups, and how they differ in experience of the problem (`targetGroups`).

## 2. Goal and what you answer for

- The long-term change the work is for (`outcomes` at level `impact`).
- The ceiling of accountability: what you answer for, and what you only contribute to (`ceilingOfAccountability`).

## 3. Pathways of change

- One pathway per program area or strategy (`pathways`), drawn as a diagram.
- For each outcome: the level, who changes, and who delivers it (`outcomes`, `responsibility`, `deliveredBy`).
- For each link: why the first change makes the second more likely (`links.rationale`).
- Where several things lead to one outcome, whether all, any, or some are needed (`logic`, `preconditionGroups`).

## 4. Interventions

- What is done, by whom, and which outcomes it brings about (`interventions`).
- Why these and not others (`priority`).

## 5. Assumptions and risks

- What must be true for each link and intervention to work, and how it will be tested (`assumptions`).
- What could go wrong, and what is done about it (`risks`, `externalFactors`).
- Assumptions about partners and about the context, not only about the organization.

## 6. Evidence

- What supports the main claims, and what is only the authors' reading (`evidence`, `provenance`).
- Where the evidence is thin, and what would settle it.

## 7. Actors

- Who is involved, what each does, and their stance (`actors`).

## 8. Measurement

- How each outcome the organization answers for is measured: baseline, target, source, frequency (`indicators`).
- What measurement costs and the budget it must fit (`measurementBudget`).
- Shared definitions so one metric means one thing (`metrics`, `glossary`).

## 9. Who has seen it

- Who was consulted, how, and what changed as a result (`consultations`, `revisions`).

## 10. The picture

- A diagram of the pathway and a plain-language narrative that agree with the model.
