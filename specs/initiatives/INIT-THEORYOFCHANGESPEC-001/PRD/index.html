    PRD — Impact Model Specification — Go-first model, JSON Schema, measurement, and leadership reporting¶

    Note on names. This specification was written when the product was called the Theory of Change specification. It is now the impact model, and the theory of change, the logic model, the outcome map, and the logframe are views of it. Below, "theory" means the impact model document unless it names the method. See the decision.

    Initiative: INIT-THEORYOFCHANGESPEC-001 Repository: github.com/ProductBuildersHQ/impact-model Workflow: pbhq-lite Status: Draft — pending stakeholder review (pbhq-lite gate: after PRD)

    Background research: docs/research/theory-of-change.md.

    1. Problem¶

    A Theory of Change (ToC) states how and why a desired long-term change is expected to happen: a long-term goal, the web of preconditions (outcomes) that must hold for it, the interventions that produce them, the assumptions behind every causal step, and the indicators that show whether change is happening. The method is mature in philanthropy and international development (Aspen Roundtable / ActKnowledge, Annie E. Casey Foundation, UNDG) and applies just as well to corporate and market strategy — for example, driving adoption of one of several competing standards for agentic authorization.

    Today a ToC almost always lives as a diagram in a slide deck plus a narrative document. That causes four concrete failures:

    1. Not machine-readable. Pathways, assumptions, and indicators sit in pictures and prose. No tool can check that every outcome has an indicator, that every causal link has a stated rationale, or that the pathway actually reaches the long-term goal. Current state: 0 of these checks are automated anywhere in the ProductBuildersHQ / PRISM ecosystem.
    2. Disconnected from measurement. Indicator baselines, targets, and observations are tracked in separate spreadsheets. Leadership cannot see "which part of our theory is working, which part is failing, and which assumptions have broken" without an analyst rebuilding the picture by hand each reporting cycle (typically several analyst-hours per report).
    3. Not composable. The ToC cannot be joined to the strategy artifacts PRISM already models (OKRs, V2MOMs, capabilities, maturity SLIs, market entities), so it cannot feed the planned Unified Strategy Model (USM) — ToC combined with a Balanced Scorecard from one market participant's perspective — or the planned Unified Futures Model (UFM) — several competing futures, each championed by different market actors, measured side by side (e.g. competing proposals to extend or replace OAuth for agentic access, such as AAuth, AIMS, and Poppy).
    4. Not AI-native. There is no schema an agent can author against, no deterministic validator to keep an agent honest, and no rubric to judge ToC quality, so LLM help with ToCs produces unverifiable prose.
    5. Not sustainable for small organizations. Small-staff organizations — especially intermediaries such as nonprofit networks, associations, and collective-impact backbones — run several program areas, serve member organizations that in turn serve end beneficiaries, and have a few staff hours a week for measurement. Their impact evidence is mostly anecdotal and lives in multi-tab spreadsheets with heavy manual entry. They need to pick a small metric set (typically 8–12) that is consistent across program areas, cheap to collect (passive data first, minimal surveys), honest about contribution versus attribution, and directly usable for audience-specific storytelling and grow / adjust / sunset program decisions. No current tool connects the ToC, the metric definitions, the collection burden, the stories, and the reports.

    Who is affected:

    • Strategy owners (product/portfolio leaders, program officers) who author and revise ToCs.
    • Executives and funders who need a short, trustworthy read-out of progress.
    • Analysts / evaluators who maintain indicators and test assumptions.
    • AI agents (Claude Code, MCP clients) that draft, critique, and update ToCs.
    • Downstream model builders (USM, UFM) that need a ToC building block.
    • Measurement leads at small-staff intermediary organizations who must define a unified metric framework across program areas within a fixed staff-time budget.
    • Non-technical analysts who work in spreadsheets and run repeatable steps through an AI assistant rather than a CLI.
    • Communications staff who turn metrics and stories into audience-specific narratives and annual impact reports.
    • Consultants and facilitators who run ToC workshops and need to produce the diagram, glossary, logic models, and metric set quickly.

    Desired state: a ToC is a versioned JSON (or YAML) document conforming to a published JSON Schema generated from Go structs. Go tools validate it, analyze its causal graph, roll up indicator observations into pathway status, judge its quality against a rubric, and render standard and bespoke reports — executive summary, pathway diagram, results framework, assumption register — in seconds.

    2. Goals and Non-Goals¶

    2.1 Goals¶

    ID Goal Success criterion
    G-1 Capture the canonical ToC vocabulary as a Go-first data model Every core concept in the research brief's entity model (§8) maps to a Go type and a JSON Schema definition; mapping table published in docs
    G-2 Publish a lint-clean, embedded JSON Schema Schemas generated from Go types only; schemakit lint --property-case camelCase reports 0 violations; schemas embedded via //go:embed
    G-3 Make ToC quality checkable without an LLM ≥ 15 deterministic lint rules covering structure, causal logic, and measurability, each with a stable rule ID and unit test
    G-4 Turn observations into pathway status Given a ToC and an observation set, compute indicator, outcome, assumption, and pathway status deterministically (same inputs → byte-identical output)
    G-5 Generate leadership-ready reports Executive summary, pathway diagram (Mermaid), results framework, and assumption register render from one command in < 1 s for a 200-node ToC
    G-6 Make ToC authoring and review AI-native ToC spec template + structured-evaluation rubric for LLM-as-a-Judge; MCP server exposing validate / lint / status / report tools
    G-7 Reuse PRISM rather than duplicate it Shared primitives come from prism-core; OKR and outcome-journey exports go through prism-roadmap types; no parallel Person / Risk / health types
    G-8 Be a stable building block for USM and UFM A published integration contract (typed references, perspective, alignment links, actor registry); a test fixture composes three competing-future ToCs with a shared actor registry without schema changes
    G-9 Model program portfolios and two-layer (intermediary) impact A reference ToC with ≥ 3 program pathways, an organization-level impact, member-organization and end-beneficiary layers, and contribution-labelled downstream indicators validates with 0 schema changes
    G-10 Make measurement sustainable Shared metric definitions with fixed formulas; metric-selection scoring; per-indicator collection mode and staff-minute cost; budget checks warn when the selected set exceeds the declared staff-time or respondent-question budget
    G-11 Connect measurement to story and learning Audiences, a story theme codebook, Most-Significant-Change-style stories, and learning reviews are modelled; audience-specific story, learning-review, and portfolio-decision reports render from data
    G-12 Produce workshop-ready definition artifacts before any data exists Pathway diagram, per-pathway logic models, glossary, metric-selection matrix, results-framework definitions, and collection plan render from a ToC document alone
    G-13 Work where non-technical staff work CSV import of observations and CSV export of definition tables; packaged agent skills let an AI assistant run each step through the deterministic tools
    G-14 Change the theory safely through the CLI, SDK, and MCP Every edit goes through one patch engine that applies atomically and rejects edits introducing validation errors; ergonomic impact-model <element> add/set/rm commands; canonical formatting for readable diffs; semantic theory diffs that draft revision-log entries
    G-15 Produce presentation-ready artifacts Marp slide decks (workshop, executive, learning review, audience story) generated from the same report view as the Markdown reports, with optional SVG / PDF / PPTX export
    G-17 Draft a credible ToC from public sources, honestly labelled Every element carries a basis (stated, inferred, hypothesis, confirmed, rejected) and source citations; reports and decks mark inferred elements; an assessment report lists gaps and questions for the organization

    G-16 Make LLM-as-a-Judge results reproducible and trustworthy Judge bundles and verdicts are files alongside the ToC; multi-judge runs record inter-rater agreement; lint profiles keep early drafts from drowning in warnings

    2.2 Non-Goals¶

    • Building USM or UFM. This initiative defines the ToC building block and its integration contract only. Balanced Scorecard modeling, futures comparison, and market-share forecasting belong to those initiatives.
    • A diagram editor or web UI. Output is files (Markdown, Mermaid, JSON, HTML via existing PRISM/site tooling later). Visual editing is out of scope.
    • Causal inference or statistics. We record evidence strength and compute rule-based confidence; we do not estimate effect sizes, run counterfactuals, or claim attribution.
    • Data collection. Observations are supplied as files (JSON, YAML, or CSV); connectors to CRMs, form tools, analytics systems, survey tools, or GitHub are out of scope. Record linkage (matching sign-ups to contacts and organizations) and survey administration happen upstream; this module consumes the resulting aggregate observations.
    • Automated theme coding. The module defines the theme codebook and validates coded stories; assigning themes to free text (by people or an LLM) happens outside it.
    • Google (or any cloud) API dependencies inside this module. Sharing to Google Drive and reading responses back use external tooling invoked by an agent skill; this module produces and consumes files only.
    • LLM calls inside this module. The module ships templates, rubrics, and prompts; judging is executed by visionspec / visionstudio or an MCP client. No model SDKs become dependencies of this repo.
    • Persistence / multi-user storage. No database. Documents live in git.
    • Logframe-only workflows. The canonical model is the ToC outcomes framework. Per-pathway logic-model views are projections of it (PRD-FR-505); logframe matrices may come later.

    3. Personas and User Stories¶

    Acceptance criteria are written to be testable by unit tests, golden files, or CLI exit codes.

    US-1 — Author a ToC as data. As a strategy owner, I want to write my ToC in YAML or JSON against a published schema, so that editors give me completion and errors as I type.

    • Given a document with $schema set to the published schema URL, VS Code / any JSON-Schema-aware editor validates it (manual check, documented).
    • impact-model validate my.impact-model.yaml exits 0 for a valid document and non-zero with JSON-pointer locations for each schema or reference error.

    US-2 — Catch weak theories early. As a strategy owner, I want an automated critique of my ToC's logic, so that gaps (outcomes with no indicator, links with no rationale, pathways that never reach the goal) are found before review.

    • impact-model lint reports each finding with rule ID, severity, element ID, and a fix hint; output is a structured-evaluation GO/NO-GO summary report in JSON or a human table.
    • A seeded-defect fixture triggers every lint rule exactly once.

    US-3 — See which part of the theory is working. As an executive, I want a one-page summary of progress toward the long-term goal, so that I know where the theory is holding, where it is failing, and what decision is needed.

    • impact-model report exec produces Markdown containing: overall status, pathway status by level, top at-risk outcomes, broken or untested critical assumptions, changes since the previous period, and decisions requested.
    • Every status in the report traces to an indicator observation or an assumption assessment ID (no unsupported claims).

    US-4 — Update measurements without editing the theory. As an analyst, I want to record observations in a separate file per period, so that the slow-changing theory and the fast-changing measurements have independent git histories.

    • Observation sets reference indicators by ID; impact-model status merges any number of observation files; references to unknown indicators are errors.

    US-5 — Test assumptions explicitly. As an evaluator, I want each critical assumption to carry its test, its evidence, and a current assessment, so that broken assumptions automatically flag every downstream outcome as at risk.

    • Marking an assumption invalidated changes the pathway status of every outcome that depends on the links it underpins, verified by unit test.

    US-6 — Let an agent do the drafting. As a strategy owner working with an AI agent, I want the agent to draft and revise my ToC through tools that validate every edit, so that agent output is always schema-valid and lint-checked.

    • MCP server exposes impact_validate, impact_lint, impact_graph, impact_status, impact_report, and impact_schema; each returns structured JSON; an MCP integration test drives every tool.
    • A ToC spec template and rubric are published so visionspec / visionstudio can run LLM-as-a-Judge on a ToC narrative.

    US-7 — Connect the theory to plans. As a portfolio leader, I want ToC outcomes and indicators exported as OKRs and interventions linked to PRISM capabilities, so that execution plans trace back to the theory.

    • impact-model export okr emits a prism-roadmap OKR document that passes prism-roadmap's own validation.
    • Interventions reference capabilities with prism-core CapabilityRef.

    US-8 — Compare competing futures (contract only). As a UFM model builder, I want several ToCs — each describing one competing future from its champions' perspective — to share an actor registry and be referenced by stable IDs, so that UFM can compare them without each ToC knowing about the others.

    • Fixture: three ToCs for three competing approaches share one actor registry; all three validate; cross-document references resolve via the published reference format.

    US-9 — One theory, several program areas. As a measurement lead at an intermediary organization, I want one organization-level ToC with a pathway (and logic model) per program area, so that each program keeps its own outcomes while everything rolls up to the organization's impact.

    • Pathways group outcomes, interventions, and indicators; reports filter by --pathway; organization-level indicators roll up from pathway indicators where the metric's roll-up rule allows it.
    • A program may instead be its own ToC document whose outcomes contribute to outcomes in a parent ToC via references; both styles validate.

    US-10 — Honest about two layers of impact. As an intermediary, I want indicators for changes in member organizations marked as attributable and indicators for end beneficiaries marked as contribution, so that reports never overclaim.

    • Every indicator has a claim of attribution or contribution; reports label contribution indicators; lint warns when an indicator above the ceiling of accountability claims attribution.

    US-11 — Choose a small, sustainable metric set. As a measurement lead, I want to score candidate metrics against selection criteria and see the total collection burden of the selected set, so that we measure what matters within our staff-time budget.

    • Candidate indicators carry criterion scores and a selection status; impact-model report metric-selection ranks them; impact-model report collection-plan totals staff minutes per period and survey questions per respondent per year against the declared budget; lint warns on budget overrun.

    US-12 — Workshop artifacts on day one. As a facilitator, I want the pathway diagram, logic models, glossary, metric definitions, and collection plan generated from the draft ToC during and right after a workshop, so that the group reviews the same artifacts the system will later measure against.

    • All definition reports render with zero observations; CSV export of each definition table opens in a spreadsheet.

    US-13 — Data from spreadsheets, stories from people. As a non-technical analyst, I want to load monthly numbers from a CSV and record coded stories, so that I never hand-edit JSON.

    • impact-model import observations --csv maps columns to indicators and periods and reports unmatched rows; stories carry themes from the document's codebook, consent and sensitivity; theme counts become qualitative indicator values.

    US-14 — Learn and tell the story. As a leadership team, I want a quarterly learning-review draft, audience- specific impact stories, and a portfolio view of each program's progress, burden, and confidence, so that we learn, communicate, and decide to grow, adjust, or sunset programs from the same data.

    • impact-model report learning-review, impact-model report story --audience <id>, and impact-model report portfolio render from the ToC, observations, and stories; every number cites its source; restricted stories are never quoted.

    US-15 — An assistant runs the routine. As a non-technical analyst using an AI assistant, I want packaged agent skills that call the deterministic tools in a fixed order with fixed definitions, so that monthly results are identical no matter which run or model produced them.

    • Skills for authoring a ToC, refreshing status from CSV, drafting a learning review, and drafting audience stories; each skill states its inputs, commands, checks (row counts, unmatched rows, change versus previous run), and outputs.

    US-16 — Edit the theory without hand-editing JSON. As a strategy owner or AI agent, I want to add, change, and remove outcomes, links, assumptions, indicators, and actors with commands or small patches that are validated before they are written, so that the document is never left in a broken state and every change is a readable diff.

    • impact-model patch applies an RFC 6902 JSON Patch atomically; if the result has more error-severity validation issues than the input, nothing is written and the new issues are reported.
    • impact-model outcome add --id … --title … --level …, impact-model link add --from … --to …, and siblings for every element kind produce the same result as the equivalent patch.
    • impact-model fmt rewrites a document in canonical form; running it twice is a no-op.
    • impact-model diff old new lists added / removed / changed elements by ID and can emit a draft Revision entry.

    US-17 — Present it. As a facilitator or executive sponsor, I want slide decks generated from the ToC and its status, so that workshops, board meetings, and funder briefings use the same numbers as the reports.

    • impact-model deck workshop|exec|learning-review|story writes Marp Markdown; with the optional Marp and Mermaid CLIs installed, --export pdf|pptx|html produces a finished file, and diagrams render to SVG.

    US-18 — Judge quality reproducibly. As a reviewer, I want the judge input and each judge's verdict stored as files, and agreement across judges reported, so that a rubric score is auditable rather than a one-off chat answer.

    • impact-model judge show emits the judge bundle (rubric, ToC JSON, generated narrative, lint summary); impact-model judge record validates and stores a structured-evaluation verdict; impact-model judge summary aggregates several verdicts with agreement metrics.

    US-19 — Draft from public sources. As a consultant or analyst, I want an assistant to draft an organization's ToC from its public website and reports, with every element labelled as stated by the organization or inferred by us and cited to its source, so that the draft is a credible, honest conversation starter.

    • impact-model evidence add --url … --title … --excerpt … --accessed … records sources; the impact-draft-from-sources skill creates evidence records and builds the draft only through validated patches; lint reports elements without a basis and stated elements without a citation.
    • impact-model report assessment lists stated vs inferred elements, outcomes with no indicator, indicators whose data is not available, assumptions needing testing, and generated questions for the organization.

    US-20 — Review without touching the tools. As a staff member at the organization, I want to review the draft as a familiar document or spreadsheet in Google Drive and mark each element agree / change / remove / unsure with comments, so that my feedback reaches the model without learning a new tool.

    • impact-model export review-packet produces Markdown that Pandoc converts to DOCX, with one ID-tagged table per element kind and empty response columns; impact-model export review-sheet produces the same rows as CSV.
    • Every exported artifact carries the ToC ID, version, and content hash.
    • Responses read back (by a person or an assistant using Google tooling outside this module) are normalized into a ReviewResponse file; impact-model import review turns it into a proposed patch and a consultation record. Nothing is applied until someone runs impact-model patch on the proposal. Responses against a different content hash are flagged.

    US-21 — Agree on the baseline. As an organization's leadership, I want to approve the reviewed ToC as a named version, so that both teams work from the same agreed theory.

    • impact-model approve --version v1.0 --approver … sets status approved, records approvers and a Revision, and refuses while any error lint finding or unresolved hypothesis element remains at an accountable level unless --force with a reason.
    • impact-model diff hypothesis.json v1.0.json shows what changed through review, including rejected elements.

    4. Functional Requirements¶

    4.1 Model (PRD-FR-1xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-101 The model MUST represent a ToC document with: identity and metadata, perspective (the actor whose theory it is), scope and context (problem statement, root causes, target population or market, time horizon), one or more impact-level goals, and a ceiling of accountability.
    • PRD-FR-102 The model MUST represent outcomes as graph nodes with a level (impact, long_term, medium_term, short_term, output), a sphere (control, influence, interest), an open category taxonomy (e.g. ORS impact / influence / leverage, COM-B, market-systems Adopt–Adapt–Expand– Respond), and a precondition logic (all, any, or at_least k-of-n) over their incoming links, with explicit, nestable precondition groups for combinations such as (A AND B) OR C.
    • PRD-FR-103 The model MUST represent causal links as directed edges with type, rationale, linked assumptions, linked evidence, interventions placed on the link, and an optional feedback flag (reinforcing / balancing) that permits intentional loops such as network effects.
    • PRD-FR-104 The model MUST represent interventions (strategies / activities) with the outcomes they target, their outputs, owning actor, status, and optional capability and initiative references.
    • PRD-FR-105 The model MUST represent assumptions with type (causal, contextual, implementation, behavioral, external, partnership), an optional structured if / then / because statement, criticality, plausibility, whether testing is needed, the elements they apply to, how they will be tested, and a current status (the UNDG three questions: complete? plausible? needs testing?).
    • PRD-FR-106 The model MUST represent indicators attached to outcomes, intervention outputs, or assumptions, carrying ActKnowledge's four components (population, target, threshold, timeline) plus definition, unit, direction, baseline, time-phased targets / milestones, data source, collection method, frequency, owner, population, and measurement type (quantitative, qualitative scale, or hybrid).
    • PRD-FR-107 The model MUST represent actors (stakeholders, boundary partners, beneficiaries, champions, competitors) with type, role, stance, influence, and interest.
    • PRD-FR-108 The model MUST represent evidence (source, type, strength, sensitivity) and external factors / risks, reusing prism-core types where they exist; risks carry a category (environmental_political, opportunity, design, partnership, market, technical, operational) and the assumptions they mirror.
    • PRD-FR-109 The model MUST represent alignments from any element to an external framework element (OKR, Balanced Scorecard objective, V2MOM method, PRISM capability, maturity SLI, market-spec entity, USM, UFM, SDG, custom).
    • PRD-FR-110 The model MUST record a revision log (version, summary, trigger) to support adaptive management.

    • PRD-FR-111 Observations MUST live in a separate observation-set document type referencing indicators and assumptions by ID, holding quantitative values, qualitative levels, assumption assessments, timestamps, sources, and notes.

    • PRD-FR-112 The model MUST represent pathways (named groupings such as program areas or strategies) over outcomes, interventions, and indicators, and MAY reference a parent ToC whose outcomes this document's outcomes contribute to.

    • PRD-FR-113 The model MUST represent shared metric definitions (name, definition, exact formula, unit, aggregation / roll-up rule) that indicators instantiate per pathway, so one metric is computed identically everywhere.
    • PRD-FR-114 Every indicator has a claim (attribution or contribution; contribution when omitted, so reports never overclaim) and MAY declare caveats (e.g. "based on sign-ups, not attendance") that reports must render.
    • PRD-FR-115 The model MUST represent metric selection (configurable criteria, per-indicator scores, status candidate / selected / retired, rationale) and collection cost (mode: passive, form, survey, interview, manual, derived; source system; staff minutes per period; questions per respondent), plus a document-level measurement budget.
    • PRD-FR-116 The model MUST represent audiences (who, what they need to see) and allow outcomes, indicators, and stories to target audiences.
    • PRD-FR-117 The model MUST represent a glossary and a story theme codebook (theme ID, label, definition, examples, related outcomes).

    • PRD-FR-118 Observation sets MUST additionally carry stories (summary, optional quote, date, actor, domain of change, themes, related outcomes, consent, sensitivity), learning reviews (period, questions, findings, decisions, actions), and per-observation data quality (verified, reported, estimated).

    • PRD-FR-119 The model MUST represent a problem tree: causes with level (immediate, underlying, structural), parent cause, and affected groups; outcomes MUST be able to declare which causes they address, so the solution tree can be checked against the problem tree at every level.

    • PRD-FR-120 Outcomes MUST declare responsibility (own, shared, partner, unaddressed) and the actors delivering them. All outcomes are monitored; only own and shared outcomes count toward the perspective actor's accountability.
    • PRD-FR-121 Prioritization criteria (e.g. equity, comparative advantage, feasibility, evidence of what works) MUST be scorable on outcomes and interventions as well as indicators, with the subject kind declared on each criterion.
    • PRD-FR-122 The model MUST represent target groups (priority populations) in the context and let indicators declare the target groups they disaggregate by.

    • PRD-FR-123 The model MUST record consultations / validations: date, ToC step validated (focus, change_analysis, assumptions_risks, actors, measurement, whole), method (workshop, interview, survey, document review, evidence review), stakeholder groups (not individuals), findings, and resulting changes.

    • PRD-FR-124 Every element (outcome, group, link, intervention, assumption, indicator, metric, actor, cause, pathway, risk, external factor) MUST support provenance: basis (stated, inferred, hypothesis, confirmed, rejected), cited evidence IDs, and a note. Rejected elements remain in the document (excluded from graph analysis, measurement, and default reports) so the review history stays visible.

    • PRD-FR-125 Indicators MUST declare data availability: public, internal_existing, internal_new, partner_held, or unknown.

    4.2 Schema (PRD-FR-2xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-201 JSON Schemas MUST be generated from the Go types; no hand-written schema files.
    • PRD-FR-202 Generated schemas MUST be embedded and retrievable by name at runtime and via impact-model schema <name>.
    • PRD-FR-203 Documents MAY be authored in YAML; YAML MUST be converted to JSON before schema validation so both formats validate identically.
    • PRD-FR-204 Observations MUST be importable from CSV through a declared column mapping, with unmatched rows reported (never dropped silently); definition tables (indicators, metrics, selection, collection plan, glossary) MUST be exportable as CSV.

    4.3 Validation, graph analysis, and lint (PRD-FR-3xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-301 Validation MUST check schema conformance, ID uniqueness, ID format, and resolution of every internal reference.
    • PRD-FR-302 Graph analysis MUST compute: upstream/downstream sets, every path from an element to the impact goal(s), topological levels, unintended cycles (excluding feedback links), orphans, and dead ends.
    • PRD-FR-303 Lint MUST implement deterministic quality rules derived from the "plausible, doable, testable" criteria and the research brief, each with a stable ID (TOC-<AREA>-<NNN>), severity, and fix hint.
    • PRD-FR-304 Lint output MUST be a structured-evaluation summary report (GO / NO-GO) so it plugs into existing ecosystem gates.

    4.4 Measurement (PRD-FR-4xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-401 Indicator progress MUST be computed direction-aware from baseline, current value, and target, with expected progress interpolated from time-phased targets at an asOf date.
    • PRD-FR-402 Indicator status MUST be one of achieved, on_track, at_risk, off_track, not_started, no_data, or stale (no observation within the indicator's frequency window), mapped to prism-core health.
    • PRD-FR-403 Outcome status MUST roll up from its own indicators and, separately, a pathway status MUST roll up from preconditions using the outcome's precondition logic.
    • PRD-FR-404 Assumption status MUST propagate: an invalidated or at_risk assumption marks the links it underpins and every downstream outcome's pathway status accordingly.
    • PRD-FR-405 Each causal link and each path to impact MUST receive a rule-based confidence score from evidence strength and assumption status, documented and reproducible.
    • PRD-FR-406 The computed status snapshot MUST itself be a schema-defined JSON document, diffable across periods ("what changed since last period").

    4.5 Reporting (PRD-FR-5xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-501 Standard reports MUST include: executive summary, pathway diagram (Mermaid flowchart with status styling), results framework table, assumption register, intervention status, and evidence-gap list.
    • PRD-FR-502 Bespoke reports MUST be possible through user-supplied Go text/template files over a documented report view model.
    • PRD-FR-503 Evidence marked restricted MUST NOT be rendered verbatim in any report; it is cited by ID with an access note.
    • PRD-FR-504 Reports MUST be deterministic for fixed inputs and asOf.
    • PRD-FR-505 Definition reports MUST render from a ToC document with no observations: per-pathway logic model (inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact projection), results-framework definitions, metric selection matrix, collection plan with budget totals, and glossary.
    • PRD-FR-506 An audience story report MUST combine the indicators and stories tagged for one audience into a narrative scaffold with every number cited; stories without consent or marked restricted MUST NOT be quoted.
    • PRD-FR-507 A learning-review report MUST draft the period's review: status changes, surprises (largest deviations from expected progress), assumption changes, theme counts, and open questions.
    • PRD-FR-508 A portfolio report MUST show, per pathway, pathway status, confidence, collection burden, and audience coverage, as input to grow / adjust / sunset decisions (the report presents evidence; it does not recommend).
    • PRD-FR-510 A narrative report MUST scaffold a programme / strategy document from the ToC: causal analysis (problem tree and how outcomes address it), priorities and rationale (prioritization scores), assumptions and risks with mitigation, partner-delivered and unaddressed elements, implementation arrangements (who does what), M&E provisions (assumptions needing testing, monitoring cadence, evaluation questions from evidence gaps), and key messages.
    • PRD-FR-511 A division-of-labour report MUST show actors × outcomes / interventions with roles, flagging outcomes needing collaboration (≥ 2 delivering actors) and outcomes with no delivering actor.
    • PRD-FR-512 An assessment report MUST render from the document alone: provenance mix (counts and lists of stated / inferred / hypothesis / confirmed / rejected), outcomes without indicators, indicators by data availability, assumptions needing testing, open decisions, and generated questions for the organization (one per inferred or hypothesis element at an accountable level, plus one per gap), with a short definitions-only narrative summary.
    • PRD-FR-513 The tools MUST project the causal graph into the artifacts Theory of Change practice names, each rendered from the document alone, so a view can never disagree with the model: the pathway of change (the causal diagram, whole and per pathway), the outcome map or outcomes framework (outcomes by level, with sphere, category, responsibility, delivering actors, and what each is measured by, per pathway), the logic model (inputs to impact, as a table and as a left-to-right diagram), and the logframe matrix (goal, purpose, outputs, activities against narrative, indicators, means of verification, and assumptions). Flattening loses the links and the "why", so every projection says it is a view and points back to the element IDs.
    • PRD-FR-509 Indicators with a baseline and no target MUST report baseline_only rather than a failure, so a first measurement year is presented as establishing a baseline.

    4.6 AI-native and ecosystem integration (PRD-FR-6xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-601 The module MUST publish a ToC spec template (Markdown) and a structured-evaluation RubricSet for LLM-as-a-Judge review, embedded and syncable into specification-workflow-spec. The core rubric MUST cover the sector-neutral items of the UNDG quality-assurance checklist (participatory process, logical flow without leaps of faith, root causes addressed, explicit assumptions and risks, who does what, evidence and its limits, changes specified for target groups, diagram plus narrative); an optional development rubric profile MUST add the sector-specific items (gender, leaving no one behind, resilience to shocks, poverty–environment links, structural causes of violence).
    • PRD-FR-602 An MCP server (official Go SDK) MUST expose validate, lint, graph, status, report, and schema tools over stdio.
    • PRD-FR-603 Export to prism-roadmap OKR documents and outcome journeys MUST be provided in an adapter package that the core model does not import.
    • PRD-FR-604 A published reference format MUST identify a ToC document and any element within it across repositories, for use by USM and UFM.
    • PRD-FR-605 The repository MUST ship agent skills (instruction files plus helper templates) for: authoring / revising a ToC, refreshing status from CSV, drafting a learning review, and drafting audience stories. Skills MUST delegate every computation to the deterministic CLI or MCP tools.
    • PRD-FR-606 The repository MUST ship a impact-draft-from-sources skill: given source URLs or documents, it records evidence with excerpts, drafts elements through patches with provenance, and renders the workshop pack and assessment report.
    • PRD-FR-607 The repository MUST ship a toc-share-google skill that exports the review packet, review sheet, and decks, publishes them with external Google tooling (an SDK CLI or an MCP server, not a dependency of this module), reads responses back (response columns, comments, and suggestions), writes a ReviewResponse, and runs impact-model import review. The skill MUST also document a manual fallback (download/upload by hand, CSV review sheet).

    4.7 Editing (PRD-FR-7xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-701 All document mutations (CLI, SDK, MCP) MUST go through one patch engine applying RFC 6902 operations atomically, with element addressing by ID (e.g. /outcomes/@oc-adoption/title) as well as by index.
    • PRD-FR-702 A patch MUST be rejected (nothing written) when it increases the number of error-severity validation issues, unless --allow-errors is given; the response lists issues introduced and resolved.
    • PRD-FR-703 Element commands (add, set, rm, plus link/unlink where relevant) MUST exist for outcomes, groups, links, interventions, assumptions, indicators, metrics, pathways, actors, evidence, causes, and glossary terms; removing an element MUST report (and with --cascade, remove) references to it.
    • PRD-FR-704 impact-model fmt MUST produce a canonical serialization (field order, indentation, element order preserved unless --sort), idempotent, for JSON and YAML.
    • PRD-FR-705 impact-model diff MUST report a semantic diff between two ToC versions keyed by element ID and MAY emit a draft Revision entry.
    • PRD-FR-706 The MCP server MUST expose impact_patch using the same engine.
    • PRD-FR-707 impact-model approve MUST be the only path to status approved: it records approvers (prism-core Approver), a Revision with trigger review, and the version label, and enforces the checks in US-21.

    4.9 Sharing and review round trip (PRD-FR-9xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-901 A pandoc Markdown profile MUST be available for every report and the review packet: diagrams pre-rendered to PNG, pipe tables without nested formatting, a YAML metadata block (title, version, date), and explicit page breaks; impact-model export docx|pptx MUST wrap an optional external pandoc with an optional reference template, from the CLI only.
    • PRD-FR-902 Every exported artifact (Markdown, DOCX, PPTX, CSV, deck) MUST carry an export stamp: ToC ID, version label, content hash (SHA-256 of canonical JSON), and as-of date.
    • PRD-FR-903 impact-model export review-packet and impact-model export review-sheet MUST list elements by kind with stable element ID, statement, basis, sources, and empty response columns decision (agree / change / remove / unsure), suggested_change, and comment.
    • PRD-FR-904 A schema-defined ReviewResponse MUST capture: ToC ID, version, content hash, reviewer group (not individual names required), method, and rows (element ID, decision, suggested change, comment, origin: sheet, table, comment, suggestion, plus quoted anchor text).
    • PRD-FR-905 impact-model import review MUST validate a ReviewResponse, produce a proposed patch (agree → basis confirmed; remove → basis rejected; change → field edits when the suggested change is structured, otherwise a note flagged for human decision), and a Consultation record; it MUST NOT modify the document, and MUST flag responses whose content hash differs from the current document.

    4.8 Presentations and judging (PRD-FR-8xx)¶

    • PRD-FR-801 Slide decks MUST be generated as Marp Markdown from the report view model: workshop (problem, pathway, logic models, assumptions, metric definitions, glossary, open questions), exec, learning-review, and story (per audience).
    • PRD-FR-802 Diagram and deck export (Mermaid → SVG, Marp → PDF / PPTX / HTML) MUST be optional and performed by external CLIs invoked from the impact-model command only; the library emits text and stays network- and binary-free.
    • PRD-FR-803 impact-model judge show | record | summary MUST produce the judge bundle, validate and store structured-evaluation verdicts beside the document, and aggregate multiple verdicts with inter-rater agreement.
    • PRD-FR-804 Lint MUST support profiles: draft (errors and structural rules only), standard (default), and full (all rules including info), so a minimal valid document — goal, one outcome, one link, one indicator — lints GO under draft.

    6. Assumptions and Risks¶

    ID Assumption / Risk Impact if wrong Mitigation
    A-1 The canonical ToC vocabulary (Aspen/ActKnowledge, AECF, UNDG) is stable enough to freeze in a v0 schema Breaking schema changes after adopters start Version the schema ($id with major version); additive-only within v0.x minors; revision log
    A-2 Rule-based confidence scoring is useful without statistics Leaders over-trust a number Label scores as "rule-based confidence", show inputs, never call it attribution
    A-3 USM and UFM can be built on top of the reference format and alignment links without changing the ToC model Rework when USM/UFM land UFM fixture in Phase 5; contract reviewed with USM/UFM owners before v1
    A-4 Non-technical users will reach the tools through an AI assistant with agent skills and spreadsheets (CSV), not the CLI directly Tools unused by the people who most need them Skills and CSV paths are first-class requirements (PRD-FR-204, 605); success metric for skill determinism
    A-5 A per-indicator staff-minute estimate is a good-enough proxy for collection burden Budget checks mislead Estimates are labelled as estimates; actual time logs can be recorded as observations of a burden indicator and compared
    R-1 prism-roadmap pulls a large dependency tree Heavier builds for core users Keep adapters in a separate package; core packages import only prism-core and the standard library plus schema tooling
    R-2 Over-modeling makes authoring painful Low adoption Minimal valid document is small (goal + one outcome + one link + one indicator); most fields optional; draft lint profile; edit commands; templates and examples
    R-3 YAML comments are lost when tools rewrite a document Authors lose notes Documented: tool-written files are canonical; notes belong in description / narrative fields; --dry-run shows the patch result before writing

    7. Out of Scope / Future¶

    • USM (ToC + Balanced Scorecard from a market participant's perspective) and UFM (competing futures with actor coalitions) — separate initiatives that consume this module.
    • Logframe matrix and IATI results exports.
    • Web viewer (likely via prism sitegen or the @grokify/prism viewer).
    • Data connectors (CRMs, form tools, analytics, surveys, GitHub, visionstudio delivery evidence as automatic output indicators) and record linkage.
    • XLSX import/export (CSV covers spreadsheets for v0.1) and hosted dashboards.
    • A hosted multi-organization service for intermediaries.
    • Probabilistic or Bayesian scoring of pathways; contribution-analysis workflows beyond the evidence and assumption records.
    • GTM and growth-phase artifacts for the tool itself.
