    Measuring Progress¶

    An impact model says what you expect. Measuring says how far you have come. The measurement is a pure function of three inputs: the impact model, the observations, and a date. The same inputs always give the same answer.

    Indicators¶

    An indicator says how an outcome (or an assumption) is measured. It carries a baseline, targets with dates, a collection frequency, and the direction that counts as progress (increase or decrease). A quantitative indicator has numbers; a qualitative one has ordered levels, such as Emerging to Embedded.

    Indicators can use a shared metric from the library, so the same measure is defined once. A metric says how values combine across pathways: sum, mean, weighted_mean, median, mode, min, max, latest, or none for a count that cannot be added up, such as distinct people. An indicator can also roll up from other indicators; a direct observation always beats a roll-up.

    An indicator's claim says what the organization can say about it: attribution (we caused this) or contribution (we contributed to it). The default is contribution.

    Where an indicator sits in the chain¶

    What an indicator measures decides its tier, which is never stored:

    Tier The indicator measures For example
    activity an intervention hours delivered, dollars spent
    output an output-level outcome people reached
    outcome a short-term, medium-term, or long-term outcome skills gained, practice applied. These are the driver metrics
    impact an impact outcome residents served, reported as a contribution

    Resources are measured as activity-tier indicators with a unit such as hours or dollars. impact-model report metric-tree lays a pathway's indicators out in this order.

    North Star, guardrails, and headline lists¶

    Three choices sit on top of the tiers, and they are different things:

    • A North Star is the one number a program or product steers by. Set it in the pathway's focus, with an optional oneMetricId and the guardrailIds that watch what pursuing it could harm. It should measure an outcome at or below the ceiling of accountability, never an output or an activity.
    • A headline list is what an audience is told: a short, ordered list on the audience, each entry an indicator with an optional label and note worded for that reader. Every audience sees the same figure for an indicator; only the label and note differ. A North Star may lead one list and not appear in another.
    • Purpose is context.mission and context.vision, plain statements. They are not part of the causal chain.

    impact-model report headline shows what each audience is told, and the story report and deck use the same lists. impact-model lint checks all of it: the TOC-NSM-*, TOC-HLN-*, IMP-AUD-002, and IMP-CTX-001 rules.

    Working a metric out from others¶

    Some of the numbers people ask for are arithmetic on others, such as the cost per applied change. Give that indicator a composedOf:

    composedOf:
  op: ratio              # ratio, difference, product, or sum
  indicatorIds: [ind-cost, ind-changes-applied]   # cost divided by changes

    A ratio and a difference take exactly two indicators, in order. When the indicator has no observation of its own, its value is worked out from the latest value of each part, and it is marked as an estimate if any part is. If a part is missing, or a divisor is zero, there is no value and nothing is guessed. A composition is arithmetic, not a causal link, and an indicator is either composed or rolled up, not both.

    Observations¶

    Measurements go in an observation set, a separate file that refers to the Impact model by ID:

    kind: ObservationSet
specVersion: "0.1"
modelId: riverbend-network
period: 2026-Q3
observations:
  - indicatorId: ind-hours-learning
    date: "2026-09-30"
    value: 3650
    source: Attendance sheets
    quality: verified

    An observation has a date and either a value (quantitative) or a level (qualitative), plus a source, a quality (verified, reported, or estimated), and an optional note. The same file also holds:

    • Assumption assessments: whether an assumption is still holding.
    • Stories: a person's account, with the consent that was given and a sensitivity. See consent.
    • Learning reviews: what changed in a period, the surprises, and decisions.
    • Notes: context that belongs with the numbers.

    Check a set against the impact model it measures:

    impact-model validate observations-2026-q3.yaml --model impact-model.yaml

    Load a spreadsheet¶

    Most programs already keep their numbers in a spreadsheet. Describe its columns once in a mapping file, then import:

    modelId: riverbend-network
period: 2026-Q4
dateFormat: 02/01/2006
columns:
  indicator: Measure
  date: Month end
  value: Value
  pathway: Program
  quality: Quality
  source: Source
indicatorAliases:
  Leadership practice maturity: ind-practice-maturity
metricAliases:
  Attendee hours: met-attendee-hours

    impact-model import observations --csv q4.csv --map mapping.yaml --model impact-model.yaml \
  --out obs-q4.yaml --unmatched q4-unmatched.csv

    A row can name an indicator directly, through an alias, or through a metric plus a pathway. Every row either becomes an observation or is reported as unmatched with its row number and the reason; nothing is dropped silently. Unmatched rows are written to --unmatched as a sheet you can fix and import again. The exit code is 1 when some rows were unmatched (the rest are still written).

    Status¶

    impact-model status impact-model.yaml -O obs-q3.yaml -O obs-q4.yaml --as-of 2026-12-31 --out status-q4.json

    status prints a summary. --format json prints the full snapshot, and --out writes it to a file. The date defaults to the latest observation.

    Indicator status¶

    For each indicator the snapshot gives its progress against what the targets call for by the date:

    Status Meaning
    achieved The latest value has reached the target
    on_track At or within tolerance of the expected progress
    at_risk Behind expected progress by more than the tolerance
    off_track Far behind, or moving the wrong way
    stale The newest observation is older than twice the collection frequency
    no_data Nothing observed, and the first target date has passed
    not_started Nothing yet, before the first target date
    baseline_only No targets to measure against

    --tolerance (default 0.10) sets how far behind still counts as on track.

    Outcome and pathway status¶

    An outcome's status combines its indicators (--rollup worst, the default, or mean) with the state of what leads to it. Pathway status follows the precondition logic: an all group takes its worst member, any its best, and at_least k the k-th best. A negative link, an inhibitor, caps its target at at_risk while its source is on track.

    Assumptions and confidence¶

    An assumption assessed as at risk caps every link and outcome that depends on it at at_risk; a critical assumption that is invalidated caps them at off_track. Each link gets a rule-based confidence score from its evidence, its assumptions, and whether it has a rationale. Confidence for a route to impact is derived from its links, so a weak link shows up where it matters.

    What changed¶

    Keep each snapshot and compare:

    impact-model status impact-model.yaml -O obs-q4.yaml --prev status-q3.json
impact-model report exec impact-model.yaml -O obs-q4.yaml --prev status-q3.json

    The comparison lists the indicators, outcomes, links, and assumptions whose status or confidence changed, and the elements that were added or removed.
